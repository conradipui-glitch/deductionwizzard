# Research protocol

## Procedure for every new assertion or event

1. **Capture** exact wording, who said it, when the statement was produced, where preserved, original language, page/folio if available.
2. **Classify** as contemporary observation, testimony of belief/accusation, hearsay, retrospective narrative, modern interpretation or hypothesis.
3. **Anchor** event time **separately** from source time, with calendrical convention and uncertainty interval.
4. **Link** actors, places, institutions, texts and material artefacts via typed relations tied to claims.
5. **Check dependence**: did five accounts all copy the same earlier author?
6. **Compare** competing explanations, marking supports/contradicts/compatible/unknown. A supporting claim can be simultaneously relevant to more than one model.
7. **Propose counterchecks**: which plausible discovery would contradict the front-running model? Which observation would distinguish the competitor?
8. **Reassess** after each new participant *or event*. Keep earlier versions and never delete the original record.

## Honest qualitative heat scale (planned UI)

- **Cold**: no suitable evidence / extremely dependent reconstruction (not the same as false).
- **Cool**: compatible with known events but no discriminating support.
- **Warm**: a grounded supporting claim exists, but competitors explain it too.
- **Hot**: multiple, independently sourced, discriminating supports survive clear contradictions.
- **Contradicted**: tracked on **a separate axis**, not mixed with confidence/heat.

Heat is a transparent assessment of **evidence discrimination**, not a statistical probability. Alternative views should show source reliability, relevance and independence individually. The user may revise qualitative judgments; changes are recorded, not silently overwritten.

## Comparing religious and mythological traditions

Primary research question: *Do motifs, names, historical persons and cult practices represent shared origins, cultural transmission, independently evolved patterns, or later reinterpretations?* Do **not** assume all mythological beings were people or that similar gods are identical.

Mandatory distinctions:

- **Deity** vs **historical person** vs **legendary/epic character** vs **title/office** vs **later deified ancestor**.
- **Contemporary textual attestation** vs much later recording of older oral tradition.
- **Motif similarity** vs direct borrowing vs common inheritance vs independent convergence.
- **Name similarity** vs linguistic cognates with established sound changes and transmission path.
- **Geographic reach** and plausible contact routes, including dating and material evidence.
- **Calendar correlation**: Mesoamerican Long Count, Egyptian regnal chronology, Andean/Inca traditions and BCE/CE must keep explicit method, precision and scholarly controversy.
- **Colonial-era documentation**: identify intermediaries, translators, conversion pressures and missing indigenous voices.

A proposed identity such as “deity X = historical ruler Y” must be a separate `identity_proposal` claim and have both a plausible time/place path and independently cited supporting/contradicting evidence. It cannot be established by artistic resemblance alone.

## Basic quality gates

- No asserted event without an attributed claim.
- No claim marked `attested` without a supporting citation.
- No relationship without a claim link.
- No duplicate IDs or dangling references; no circular source derivations.
- No future information in simulated participants' knowledge (future feature).
- No automatic assignment of guilt, collusion or probability.

## Citation and quotation policy

Store pointers, locators and short excerpts needed for verification, not indiscriminate copyrighted text. For each public narrative, show the original source, date, a competing interpretation and the uncertainty where material.
